About Cancer in General

What exactly is cancer?
It is a general term describing diseases in which body cells divide uncontrollably and can invade nearby tissues or spread to other parts of the body. There are more than 100 different types, each with unique causes and behaviors.
Is cancer always hereditary?
No. Only a fraction of cases have a clear genetic component. Most cases are linked to a combination of lifestyle factors, environment, and aging rather than a single inherited cause.
Is cancer contagious?
No, cancer itself cannot spread from person to person. However, certain transmissible infections, such as HPV or Helicobacter pylori bacteria, are associated with a higher risk of developing specific cancers.
Are all tumors cancerous?
No. There are benign tumors, which do not invade other tissues or spread, and malignant tumors, which do have that capacity. Only a medical professional can determine the type through diagnostic studies.

Prevention and Detection

At what age should I start getting preventive checkups?
It depends on the type of cancer and individual family history. In general, routine screenings (such as mammograms or colonoscopies) are recommended starting around ages 40–50, but a doctor may advise different ages based on your medical history. The key is not to wait for symptoms to appear before consulting a doctor.
Can I prevent cancer using healthy habits alone?
A balanced diet, regular physical activity, avoiding tobacco, and limiting alcohol significantly reduce the risk of several cancers, but they do not guarantee absolute prevention. Combining healthy habits with periodic medical checkups is the most effective strategy.
Should I still get tested if I don't have any symptoms?
Yes. Many types of cancer present no visible symptoms in their early stages, which are typically when treatment is most effective. That is why screening exams are specifically designed for individuals without symptoms.

Treatment

What are the most common cancer treatments?
The most common treatments are surgery, chemotherapy, and radiation therapy, though targeted therapies and immunotherapy are also widely used. The treatment plan depends on the type of cancer, its stage, and the patient's overall health, and is always defined by a specialized medical team.
Is there a cure for cancer?
Many types of cancer, especially when detected early, have very high cure or remission rates. Prognosis varies widely based on type, stage, and individual response to treatment, so it is important to discuss this directly with your treating medical team.
Is it normal to feel extreme fatigue during treatment?
Yes, fatigue is one of the most common side effects during chemotherapy and radiation therapy. It is important to inform your medical team, as strategies exist to help manage it.

Emotional and Family Support

How can I help a family member who has been diagnosed?
Listening without judgment, asking what they need instead of making assumptions, and accompanying them to medical appointments if desired are usually very helpful. Visit our Psychological Support section for more practical guidance.
Is it normal to feel fear or sadness after a cancer diagnosis?
Yes, it is a completely understandable reaction. Many people experience a wide range of emotions throughout the process, and there is no single "right" way to feel. Seeking specialized psychological support can help process these emotions in a healthy manner.
Where can I look for free psychological support?
In El Salvador, there are free hotlines such as ISSS #TeEscucho (7071-1302, available 24/7) or FOSALUD. You can find complete details in our Psychological Support section.

About Canrisk

Does Canrisk replace a doctor's consultation?
No. Canrisk is an educational and informational platform; it does not replace diagnosis, treatment, or professional medical advice. If you experience any symptoms or have medical questions, we always recommend consulting a specialist.
Are the questionnaire and my data confidential?
Yes, responses to the questionnaire are completely anonymous and are used solely for educational and statistical purposes to improve our content.
