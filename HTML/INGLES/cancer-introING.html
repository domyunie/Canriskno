Introduction to Cancer


Cancer is not a single disease, but a broad term used to describe a group of more than 200 different diseases characterized by the uncontrolled growth and spread of abnormal cells in the body. If this process is not controlled or detected early, it can compromise the function of vital organs and spread to other parts of the body.


Understanding the basics of cellular development, risk factors, and the importance of early diagnosis is the fundamental first step toward prevention, comprehensive patient support, and reducing associated risks.

🎗️

Globally, cancer is responsible for nearly 1 in 6 deaths, but early detection and access to treatment significantly increase survival rates.

How Does It Start?

It begins when cellular DNA control mechanisms undergo mutations. Instead of aging and dying, these damaged cells survive and continue to divide uncontrollably, potentially forming masses known as tumors.

Prevention

Many types of cancer can be prevented or mitigated by adopting healthy habits: maintaining a balanced diet, avoiding tobacco and alcohol consumption, protecting your skin from the sun, and engaging in daily physical activity.

Early Detection

Identifying abnormalities at early stages through self-examinations, mammograms, or routine checkups significantly increases the effectiveness of current medical treatments.

Common Treatments

Depending on the type and stage of the diagnosis, treatments vary and include approaches such as surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, immunotherapy, and targeted therapies focused on stopping or eliminating affected cells.

Risk Factors

Certain factors increase the likelihood of developing cancer, such as family history, exposure to carcinogenic substances, persistent viral infections, advanced age, and certain unhealthy lifestyle choices.

Most Common Types

Among the types of cancer with the highest incidence are breast, prostate, lung, colorectal, and skin cancer. Each presents specific symptoms, risk factors, and screening protocols.

Warning Signs


Recognizing the warning signs sent by the body in time can be decisive for a timely diagnosis. No single symptom alone confirms the presence of cancer, but its persistence always warrants a medical consultation.


  • Lumps or palpable masses that do not go away over time.
  • Noticeable changes in moles, spots, or skin color.
  • Unexplained weight loss and persistent fatigue.
  • Unusual bleeding or abnormal discharge.
  • Persistent cough, hoarseness, or difficulty swallowing.
  • Changes in bowel or bladder habits.

If any sign persists for more than two weeks, don't wait: schedule an appointment with a healthcare professional. A timely diagnosis completely changes the prognosis.

Myths and Realities


Myth: Cancer is always hereditary

Reality: Only between 5% and 10% of cases are directly linked to inherited genetic mutations; the majority are due to environmental and lifestyle factors.

Myth: Sugar directly feeds cancer

Reality: All cells, healthy or cancerous, require glucose to function. Eliminating sugar alone does not cure or prevent the disease.

Myth: If there are no symptoms, there is no risk

Reality: Many types of cancer are asymptomatic in early stages, making preventive checkups essential even without visible signs.

Myth: Cancer is always a death sentence

Reality: Thanks to medical advances, many types of cancer detected early have high survival rates and effective treatment options.
