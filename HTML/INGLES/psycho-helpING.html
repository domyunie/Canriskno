Psychological Support


The diagnosis of a high-impact illness such as cancer deeply alters the emotional stability of patients and their families. The psychological impact can generate feelings of uncertainty, fear, frustration, and constant isolation.


Coping with this process requires specialized therapeutic support that validates emotions, offers practical resilience tools, and promotes a safe space of comfort where the patient does not feel alone during their adaptation process.

Professional Counseling

Psycho-oncological therapy assists patients in managing anxiety, fear, and depression arising after diagnosis, promoting a better quality of life and strengthening their mental health during treatments.

Family Support

The immediate support system also faces an overwhelming emotional load. Joint sessions guide the family in affective communication, empathy, mutual care, and collective daily stress management.

Support Groups

Sharing experiences with individuals going through similar situations alleviates the feeling of loneliness, reduces isolation, and fosters a mutual exchange of optimism and useful coping strategies.

Relaxation Techniques

Learning practical exercises in meditation, guided breathing, and emotional regulation helps reduce physical and mental tension, gradually restoring a sense of control amidst daily difficulties.

How to Offer Support with Words

A guide for family and friends: small changes in language can make a big difference

Phrases that usually help

  • "I'm here for you, whatever you need."
  • "You don't have to be strong all the time around me."
  • "How are you feeling today?" (and listening without rushing an answer)
  • "Tell me what you need, I'll take care of organizing it."
  • "I don't know exactly what to say, but I'm with you."

Phrases to avoid

  • "Everything happens for a reason" or comparing their situation to others.
  • "You have to stay positive" as the only response to their distress.
  • "I knew someone who..." followed by negative outcomes.
  • Minimizing what they feel or rushing them to "get over it soon."
  • Avoiding the topic completely, as if nothing were happening.

A Moment to Breathe

Simple breathing exercise to reduce anxiety in difficult moments

Inhale... Hold... Exhale
1. Inhale Count to 4 as air enters slowly through your nose.
2. Hold Hold the air inside while counting to 4, without forcing it.
3. Exhale Slowly release the air through your mouth while counting to 6.
4. Repeat Perform the cycle 4 to 6 times at your own pace.

Common Emotional Stages

Not everyone goes through these stages, nor in this order, nor with the same intensity. Knowing them simply helps to understand that whatever is felt is valid.

😶

Denial

Initial difficulty in processing the diagnosis news.

😠

Anger

Frustration or rage about the situation, sometimes directed at oneself or surroundings.

🤝

Bargaining

Search for meaning, internal promises, or "if only..." thoughts.

😔

Sadness

Moments of deep discouragement while processing the new reality.

🌤️

Acceptance

Progressive adaptation allowing energy to focus on the process and daily well-being.

FOSALUD

Mental health care for youth and adults, with individual or couples therapy. Also available via WhatsApp at 7556-5757.

Salvadoran Red Cross

Psychosocial Care Unit

Free and personalized intervention for stress, anxiety, deep sadness, or other emotional crises.

If you or someone close to you is having thoughts of self-harm, seek help immediately by calling one of these helplines or 911. You do not have to go through this alone.
